Nim Game (LeetCode 292): Why Multiples of 4 Lose

There's a heap of n stones. Players alternate removing 1, 2 or 3 stones, and whoever takes the last stone wins. You go first. Return whether you can win with optimal play.

Find the pattern with small cases

  • 1, 2 or 3 stones: take them all and win.
  • 4 stones: whatever you take, your opponent takes the rest. You lose.
  • 5, 6 or 7: take 1, 2 or 3 to leave your opponent at 4. You win.
  • 8: every move leaves 5 to 7, a winning spot for them. You lose.

The losing positions are exactly the multiples of 4.

def can_win_nim(n: int) -> bool:
    return n % 4 != 0

Why it works

If n is not a multiple of 4, take n % 4 stones. Now whatever your opponent removes (k stones), you remove 4 - k, keeping the pile a multiple of 4 until it hits zero on your move.

DP is the honest first draft

A boolean DP where win[i] = not all(win[i-1], win[i-2], win[i-3]) finds the pattern for you. Interviewers like seeing you derive the formula from it instead of reciting it.
